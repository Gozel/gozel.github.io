I am confused about the terminology: for me, more-than-human suggests that we start with the human at the centre and then expand our perspective to include everything around it. 

Even when the intention is to decentre humans, the human remains the reference point?
I struggle with describing an ambition by what it moves beyond, rather than what it seeks to achieve?

If the ambition is to become *fit*, we would not call it *more-than-sitting-on-the-couch*. We would describe the positive ambition: fitness, health or wellbeing.

Likewise, if the ambition is to recognise humans as part of interconnected systems, and to design with consideration for the relationships, needs and wellbeing of life as a whole, why should our terminology continue to define that ambition in relation to the human?

*More-than-human describes a shift in mindset, but not what that mindset is ultimately about?*

I like the spirit of more-than-human! I like where we're headed. This is the right direction. However, I argue, we need a terminology that expresses the positive ambition: designing for life, understanding living systems and creating conditions in which people and the wider living world can thrive.
